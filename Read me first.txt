Miller Driver Updater
=====================
(Earlier versions were called Miller Driver Update. Installing this replaces them and keeps their reports.)

What it does
  Checks this PC for driver updates (Windows Update), the newest NVIDIA GeForce driver (straight from
  nvidia.com, no NVIDIA account) and program updates (winget). You pick what to install; it makes a
  restore point and backs up each driver first; it never restarts the PC by itself.

Install (once, about 10 seconds)
  1. Double-click  MillerDriverUpdater-2.3.0-Setup.exe  and click through (Next, Next, Install).
     Or skip installing: MillerDriverUpdater.exe runs from anywhere, on its own.
     If Windows says "Windows protected your PC": click "More info", then "Run anyway". That warning
     appears for any program that is not from a big software company.
  2. It installs into your own user folder, so no administrator password is needed.
     Tick "Open Miller Driver Updater now" on the last page, or find it in the Start menu.
  3. Say Yes to the "make changes to your device" prompt when the app opens - installing drivers needs it.

  If your antivirus removes it ("MachineLearning", "Anomalous", "Generic"...): that is a guess about new
  programs, not a real detection. Setup pauses and says so: add the folder it names to the antivirus' allow list
  (Malwarebytes: Settings, Allow List, Add, Allow a file or folder), then click Retry.

Using it
  - It checks first (usually under a minute; the percentage in the headline tracks it). Nothing changes during a check.
  - Drivers come ticked; apps are listed but not ticked. Click a row to tick it, or "Check all". Click Install.
  - Apps that update themselves (Battle.net, Steam, Discord, Chrome...) sit at the bottom of the Apps list with an
    "Open" button: open them and they update on their own. They are never run from here.
  - Each row shows its own progress; the bar at the top is the whole job.
  - If it says "Restart to finish", restart when convenient. Nothing restarts on its own.
  - "Before you start" at the top lists anything likely to get in the way (on battery, low disk space,
    Windows Update service switched off...) with a button that fixes it.
  - "Needs attention" lists devices Windows cannot drive. When an update on the same screen fixes one, it says so.

If something goes wrong
  - Click "Report a Problem" (bottom right). It puts a small zip on your desktop with the app's logs -
    no personal files. Send that zip to whoever gave you the app.
  - "Roll Back" appears next to any driver this app replaced; it puts the previous one back.
  - To remove the app: Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Miller Driver Updater > Uninstall.

Where things come from
  Only official sources: Windows Update, nvidia.com (signature checked before anything runs), and
  winget. No third-party driver database, no scraping, no bundled extras.
